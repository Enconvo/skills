# McKinsey Frameworks — Working Reference

The point of these frameworks is not to fill them in. It is to **structure thinking and
force a "so what."** Use the minimum set that answers the question.

---

## 1. Hypothesis-driven approach (假设驱动)

Start with the answer you *suspect*, then test it. This is the inversion of "gather
everything, then conclude."

1. Write the **central question** as a single sentence.
2. Write **2–4 hypotheses** that, if true, answer it.
3. For each hypothesis, list the **2–3 analyses** that would confirm or kill it.
4. Do those analyses first. Update or discard hypotheses as evidence arrives.

A hypothesis must be **falsifiable**: "X is attractive because supply is constrained at
node Y" can be killed by finding spare capacity at Y. "X is interesting" cannot.

---

## 2. MECE & issue trees (问题树)

**MECE** = Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive. Every breakdown should have no
overlaps and no gaps.

Build an **issue tree** from the central question:
- Branch by structure: *Demand × Supply*, or *Revenue = Volume × Price*, or value-chain
  nodes, or geography, or customer segment.
- Each level must stay MECE.
- Leaves become the analyses you actually run.

Test for MECE: "Could a fact belong to two branches?" (not ME) or "Is there a fact that
fits no branch?" (not CE).

---

## 3. Market sizing — TAM / SAM / SOM (市场规模)

- **TAM** (Total Addressable Market): total demand if you captured 100%.
- **SAM** (Serviceable Addressable Market): the slice your product/segment can serve.
- **SOM** (Serviceable Obtainable Market): realistic near-term capture.

Always do **two independent methods and reconcile**:

**Top-down**: start from a big published aggregate, narrow by %s.
`TAM = total population/spend × % relevant × price`

**Bottom-up** (more defensible): build from units.
`TAM = # of buyers × purchase frequency × average price`

If the two disagree by >2–3×, find out why before trusting either. Always show:
the **formula, each assumption with its source, the base year, and the CAGR** with its
driver. Growth without a *mechanism* is a guess.

---

## 4. Value chain & profit pools (价值链 / 产业链 + 利润池)

List every step from raw input to end customer: **upstream → midstream → downstream**
(原材料 → 零部件/制造 → 组装 → 渠道 → 终端).

For each node estimate:

| Node | Value-add | Gross margin | Concentration (top-N / HHI) | Pricing power | Substitutability |
|------|-----------|--------------|------------------------------|---------------|-------------------|

The **profit pool** = total industry profit split by node. Plot share-of-revenue (x) vs.
margin (y); **big + high-margin = where value concentrates.** Value rarely sits where
revenue is largest — it sits where supply is scarce and switching is hard. This table is
the bridge into the bottleneck lens (see the other reference file).

---

## 5. Porter's Five Forces (五力)

Rate each force High / Medium / Low **and write the "so what."**

1. **Rivalry** — # players, concentration, growth, exit barriers, differentiation.
2. **New entrants** — capital intensity, scale economies, regulation, IP, network effects.
3. **Supplier power** — supplier concentration, switching cost, input scarcity. *(This is
   where bottlenecks show up — note it.)*
4. **Buyer power** — buyer concentration, price sensitivity, switching cost.
5. **Substitutes** — alternative technologies, price-performance trajectory.

Output is not five ratings; it is **"the structurally attractive position in this industry
is ___ because ___."**

---

## 6. PESTEL — macro drivers (宏观驱动)

Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal. List only the 1–2 factors
per category that **materially move this industry**, each with a direction and magnitude.
Drop categories that don't matter rather than padding.

---

## 7. Synthesis — Pyramid Principle (金字塔原理)

- **Answer first.** Lead with the governing conclusion.
- Support it with **3–4 grouped arguments** (MECE).
- Under each, the **evidence**.
- **SCQA** for the intro: Situation → Complication → Question → Answer.
- Every section closes with **"So what?"** — the implication for the reader's decision.

### Scenarios (情景分析)
Give **bull / base / bear** with the 1–2 swing variables that separate them, and a rough
probability or trigger for each. State what you'd watch to know which path is unfolding.

### Risks & disconfirming evidence
Name the strongest argument *against* the thesis and what observation would falsify it.
A research product with no falsification condition is marketing, not research.
